HOW TO USE · Start with a musical idea.

01 Recording → melody

AUDIO

A voice or melodic instrument can become a draft MusicXML score and MIDI file. You can specify tempo, key and the score's instrument; these choices do not isolate instruments from a mix.

If you have a recording

  1. Choose audio with a clear melody. A voice or solo instrument works better than a whole orchestra.
  2. Choose the score instrument yourself if you wish. If you do not know the tempo or key, analysis may suggest values for review; it does not identify instruments.
  3. Start transcription and wait. Download MusicXML to edit the score and MIDI to listen to the detected notes.
  4. Compare the first note, rhythm and each phrase with the recording. Correct the score in your music editor before using it.

02 PDF or image → editable notation

SCORE

Printed scores can be processed with optical music recognition. You can select a PDF page. Notes, rhythm, slurs and continuity between pages still need checking.

If you have a printed score

  1. Choose a readable PDF or image. For a PDF, you can select one page.
  2. Recognition reads symbols and creates an editable draft. Audiveris and MuseScore must be installed on your computer.
  3. Open MusicXML in MuseScore or another editor. Check pitches, rests, accidentals, slurs and meter changes against the source.

MusicXML is for editing notation; MIDI stores note events for listening but is not a replacement for the score. Maravi leaves the original file unchanged. Processing runs on your computer in a local browser page; the RASGO portal does not receive files.

  1. 01

    Listening: upload or record a melodic line.

  2. 02

    Notation: check pitch, duration and meter in MusicXML.

  3. 03

    Interpretation: refine phrasing, articulation and dynamics in an editor.

  4. 04

    Composition: develop the idea from the revised score.

The main path makes a monophonic draft; two-staff piano is experimental. Musical review remains yours.

RESEARCH AND PROGRESS

Learning to write what we hear.

The research asks how sound can become a score a person can read and play, without treating every fluctuation in the recording as another note. Local paths already exist for audio-to-melody and PDF/image-to-MusicXML/MIDI, plus tempo and key suggestions that the musician may accept or correct.

Limits guide the next work: the main audio path writes one melodic line, not separated instruments from a mix. Two-staff piano and printed-score recognition are experimental. Every output must be checked against the recording or original page.

v0.1.0 is the first public version: a package for Linux x86_64, built and tested automatically on five distributions, with the license notices of every dependency. Windows and macOS have not been tested yet. An online service that receives files is not part of this version.

ABOUT MARAVI

Notation as another way to listen.

Maravi is a device for studying, notating and organizing musical material in the RASGO family, created by Lúcio de Araújo. It is part of research into listening and musical transcription: proposing readable notation without mistaking a detector's output for the musician's intention. Its own code is available under GNU AGPLv3 or later.

Tools and credits

  • Spotify Basic Pitch (Apache 2.0): candidate notes from audio.
  • librosa (ISC): audio and tempo analysis.
  • Audiveris (AGPLv3): printed-score recognition; installed separately.
  • MuseScore Studio (GPLv3): score conversion and review; installed separately.
  • FFmpeg, Poppler and ImageMagick: local file conversion and preparation. Versions and licenses must be checked before any binaries are bundled.

Maravi did not create these tools. The Linux package includes the Python libraries (Basic Pitch, librosa and others) with their license notices; FFmpeg, Audiveris, MuseScore, Poppler and ImageMagick are installed separately, on your computer. The program's license does not change the rights of the source music, recordings or scores. The page's fonts (Fraunces, Work Sans, JetBrains Mono) are free, under the SIL OFL 1.1.
